# Priority A — core Fisher–Rao problem

## A1. Re-derive and formally verify the reduced skew optimization

### Statement

Starting from the Kobayashi Riemannian submersion and the endpoint fiber, give a clean proof of

\[
d_F^2
=
\min_{K^\top=-K}
J(K),
\]

with

\[
B=I+\Sigma+\frac12\mu\mu^\top,
\qquad
Y(K)=\frac B2+K,
\]

\[
C(K)
=
\left(
Y(K)^\top\Sigma^{-1}Y(K)
\right)^{1/2},
\]

\[
J(K)
=
2\operatorname{tr}
[
\operatorname{arcosh}^2 C(K)
].
\]

### Why this matters

This is the mathematical foundation of the entire approximation hierarchy.

### Completion criterion

A self-contained derivation with:

- correct metric scaling;
- explicit mapping between fiber parameter \(S\) and reduced skew parameter \(K\);
- proof that the minimization is exactly the Fisher–Rao distance;
- verification on exact special cases.

---

## A2. Understand the optimizer \(K_*\)

### Question

What structural constraints does the exact minimizer

\[
K_*=\arg\min_{K^\top=-K}J(K)
\]

satisfy?

In particular, is its range contained, exactly or approximately, in a low-dimensional invariant/Krylov space generated by

\[
\mu,\;
(\log\Sigma)\mu,\;
(\log\Sigma)^2\mu,\ldots?
\]

### Goal

Replace an \(O(n^2)\)-dimensional skew optimization by a problem whose dimension depends on an effective Krylov rank.

### Completion criterion

At least one of:

- exact invariant-subspace theorem;
- rigorous projection/error estimate;
- counterexample to the strongest plausible claim.

---

## A3. Prove properties of the \(d_m\) hierarchy

Given

\[
\mathcal K_m
=
\operatorname{span}
\{
\mu,A\mu,\ldots,A^{m-1}\mu
\},
\qquad
A=\log\Sigma,
\]

and

\[
d_m^2
=
\min_{K\in\Lambda^2\mathcal K_m}J(K),
\]

study:

1. monotonicity in \(m\);
2. finite termination;
3. relation to the minimal polynomial of \(A\) relative to \(\mu\);
4. convergence rate;
5. quantitative error \(d_m-d_F\).

### Obvious structural fact to exploit

The Krylov sequence stabilizes after at most \(n\) dimensions, and often much earlier if \(\mu\) has support in only a few eigenspaces of \(A\).

### Desired result

A theorem of the form

\[
0\le d_m-d_F\le \varepsilon_m(\Sigma,\mu)
\]

with an interpretable \(\varepsilon_m\).

# Priority B — understanding \(d_0\)

## B1. Derive a rigorous bad-family asymptotic

The exploratory family

\[
\Sigma_L
=
\operatorname{diag}(L^{-1},L),
\]

with a mean scaling like

\[
\mu_L=(u,v\sqrt L)
\]

suggests

\[
d_0\sim\sqrt2\log L,
\qquad
d_F\sim\log L.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{d_0}{d_F}-1
\to
\sqrt2-1.
\]

### Goal

Turn this into a precise proposition with assumptions on \(u,v\), exact asymptotic expansions, and a proof.

### Why this matters

It proves that generic excellent numerical performance of \(d_0\) does not imply a universally tiny relative error.

---

## B2. Find useful conditional bounds for \(d_0\)

A small global bound appears impossible without assumptions.

Investigate compact regimes such as

\[
\Lambda^{-1}I
\preceq
\Sigma
\preceq
\Lambda I,
\qquad
\|\mu\|\le M.
\]

### Goal

Bound

\[
d_0-d_F
\]

or

\[
\frac{d_0-d_F}{d_F}
\]

in terms of \((\Lambda,M,n)\), preferably with much better dependence than a generic compactness argument.

### Important

Do not confuse existence of a bound on a compact normalized domain with a sharp or useful bound.

---

## B3. Local expansion around easy regimes

Study \(d_0-d_F\) perturbatively when:

- \(\|\mu\|\) is small;
- \(\Sigma\) is close to \(I\);
- \([\Sigma,\mu\mu^\top]\) is small in a suitable sense;
- \(\mu\) is nearly contained in one eigenspace of \(\Sigma\).

### Desired output

An explicit leading-order error term explaining empirically when \(d_0\) is exceptionally accurate.

---

# Priority C — computational validation

## C1. Reconstruct the reference solver

Implement a high-accuracy reference for

\[
d_F
\]

using the exact skew minimization and/or an independent method.

### Requirements

- stable parameterization of skew matrices;
- gradient checks;
- multiple initializations where needed;
- agreement with exact special cases;
- documented tolerances.

No approximation should be benchmarked until this exists.

**Progress (2026-09-11).** The numerical core and mixed fourteen-case benchmark
now exist. A second objective, the lifted horizontality residual with an
analytic Frechet-log gradient, reproduces all fourteen reduced-reference
distances within 9.86e-13 after numerical qualification. See state.md section 16
and `results/horizontal_presentation_cases.md`. This experiment assumes that the
horizontal projection is minimizing; it does not close A1 or certify global
optimality. Gradient stopping alone and unresolved spectra on escaping random
restarts are demonstrated reliability issues; residual and spectral checks
are necessary in addition to BFGS success flags.

# Priority D — alternative distances

## D1. Geodesic-triangle approximation

Implement the original triangle-based candidate used in the project and record its exact formula in `notation.md`.

Then implement the symmetric modification in which the covariance used to weight the mean displacement is

\[
\Sigma_1\#\Sigma_2.
\]

### Questions

- Does the geometric-mean version improve symmetry and empirical accuracy?
- Does it preserve affine invariance?
- Is it still an upper bound, or only a heuristic?
- How does it compare with \(d_0\) computationally and numerically?

### Important

The geometric-mean replacement is a project proposal, not a literature theorem.

---

## D2. Calvo–Oller / SPD lower bound

Benchmark the ambient SPD distance induced by the Calvo–Oller embedding against:

- \(d_F\);
- \(d_0\);
- \(d_m\);
- triangle approximations.

Characterize regimes in which the lower bound is especially tight or poor.

---

## D3. Other pullback cone distances

Evaluate computationally cheap SPD-cone distances, including the Hilbert/projective construction discussed by Nielsen.

The purpose is application benchmarking, not to confuse these metrics with exact Fisher–Rao.

# Priority E — geometry of the lift

## E1. Can the Kobayashi construction be reduced below \(2n+1\)?

Kobayashi uses a symmetric space embedded in determinant-one SPD matrices of size \(2n+1\).

### Question

Is there an alternative Riemannian submersion / totally geodesic realization that preserves the needed Fisher geometry in a smaller SPD ambient dimension?

### Constraint

Any proposed reduction must preserve the metric/submersion structure, not merely give an injective embedding.

---

## E2. Clarify fiber geometry completely

Give explicit formulas for:

- the fiber \(\pi^{-1}(P)\);
- its skew parameterization;
- tangent vertical space;
- horizontal space;
- action of the isotropy group;
- relation between \(S\) and \(K\).

This should make the boundary-value reduction conceptually transparent.

---

# Publication target

The project currently aims at a publication-oriented result by the end of the year.

The strongest publishable core would likely combine:

1. a clean reduced formulation of the BVP;
2. a principled approximation hierarchy \(d_m\);
3. theory explaining effective low dimensionality and/or error;
4. convincing numerical benchmarks;
5. an application demonstrating that the approximation is useful computationally.
